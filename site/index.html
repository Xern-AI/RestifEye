Why it survives week two

Strict enough to matter. Polite enough to keep.

Most break apps get uninstalled the first time they blank the screen during a demo. RestifEye reads what you are doing before it asks for anything.

Signature

It waits for your meeting to end

Microphone live, camera live, Do Not Disturb on, a video or slide deck holding a wake lock — each one defers a due break instead of stealing your screen. A hard cap keeps deferral from quietly becoming denial.

Credits breaks you already took

Walked away, locked the screen, suspended the laptop? That was your break. RestifEye logs it as taken and never nags you for rest you already had.

24 exercises, not a blank timer

Every break shows an illustrated move — eye tracking, neck release, shoulder rolls, posture resets. Three intensity tiers, so nothing suggests a squat in an open-plan office.

Decide from the notification

A 30-second heads-up with Start now, Snooze and Skip built into the notification itself. Each has its own budget, so a snooze habit can't become a skip habit.

Advice from your own week

Screen time, idle, away, longest focus stretch and workday span — weekly, monthly, yearly. A rule engine turns four weeks of your history into concrete, judgment-free suggestions.

Private by architecture, not by promise

There is no account to make and no server to send anything to. Every second of activity data lives in a SQLite file on your machine. One optional weekly version check is the entire network story — and it has an off switch.

How it works

Out of your way, until it shouldn't be

Four moments, from login to the advice you get on Friday.

  1. 01

    It starts with your computer

    RestifEye launches at login and sits in your tray. Eye breaks every 20 minutes, movement breaks every 50 — and every interval, work-hour and work-day is yours to change.

  2. 02

    It checks the room before it asks

    When a break falls due, RestifEye reads your microphone, camera, Do Not Disturb state and any app holding an idle inhibitor. If now is wrong, it waits — up to fifteen minutes, measured from when the break was actually due.

  3. 03

    A heads-up, then a real break

    Thirty seconds of warning, always. Then one illustrated exercise, matched to the intensity you allowed. Emergencies happen: a three-second hold always lets you out, and it goes on your own record.

  4. 04

    Your patterns become your advice

    Dashboards show screen time and focus stretches. The tray face tells you how the last two hours went without opening anything at all.

Drawn, not described

All 24 exercises are drawn in code rather than shipped as images — so they stay crisp at any size, theme themselves light and dark, and add nothing to the download.

The signature detail

A face in your tray that knows how the day is going

Most break apps give you a static icon. RestifEye gives you a glanceable read on the last two hours — no window, no dashboard, no interruption.

On schedule

On schedule

  • Colour is never the only signal

    Every mood changes the eyes or the mouth too, so the icon still reads for the roughly 8% of men with a colour-vision deficiency — and in the monochrome tray themes some desktops apply.

  • Judged on a rolling two hours

    Not on day totals. A rough morning doesn't colour the icon until midnight, and a good afternoon can earn it back.

  • Praise instantly, warn slowly

    Escalation needs three consecutive samples; improvement lands immediately. One skipped break never turns the icon red.

  • Drawn at five panel sizes

    22 to 64 pixels, rendered at runtime rather than shipped as fourteen PNGs, so it never arrives as the soft rescaled smudge next to your other status icons.

FAQ

Questions, answered honestly

Does it phone home?

No. There are no accounts and no telemetry. The only network request RestifEye can make is an optional weekly version check against GitHub Releases, and you can switch it off in Settings.

Will it interrupt my presentation or call?

No. While your microphone or camera is in use, Do Not Disturb is on, or an app is holding an idle inhibitor, due breaks are deferred. A hard cap of 15 minutes — measured from when the break was actually due, not from when the deferral started — makes sure deferral can't become denial.

Can I escape a strict break in an emergency?

Always. Hold the escape button for three seconds and the break ends. It's recorded in your own stats — accountability to yourself, not to us.

Won't a long film just suppress breaks all day?

No, for two reasons. Deferral is built on idle inhibitors, which video players and slide tools take but background music does not — so a playlist can't hold breaks off. And timers are never reset on resume: two hours of video is still two hours of screen time, and the break happens as soon as the film does.

How do I know it's running?

A tray icon with a small menu — open, pause, quit — whose face changes with how your last two hours went. Closing the window keeps RestifEye running in the background, and launching it again reopens the same instance rather than starting a second one.

Wayland or X11?

Both. RestifEye is developed Wayland-first on Fedora GNOME and uses desktop-portal-friendly D-Bus APIs — no X11-only hacks that quietly stop working on modern sessions.

Is it really free?

Yes — free on Linux, forever. A paid macOS and Windows version may come later; the Linux app stays free either way.
